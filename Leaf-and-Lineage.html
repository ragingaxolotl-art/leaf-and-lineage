    YOUR POCKET FIELD GUIDE

    Inheritance, chance, and change.

    Separate row populations

    Each row has room for eight beetles and has its own cycle counter. Choose two different living parents for a row’s jar, then Breed row. Only that row changes. Empty and unfinished jars leave their rows alone. Next generation advances all complete jars once; Play keeps making new broods from their original pairs. Saved jar parents remain until you replace or remove them, including when you switch activities. Repeated broods of the same parents are siblings, not successive descendant generations. Parents may come from any row; using the same pair in all jars explicitly prepares every population.

    Surprises inherited from parents

    Several genes work together to determine color. A baby gets one copy of each gene from each parent. Those copies can pass to grandchildren and later descendants. An unusual combination can produce yellow, red, blue, or mixed colors even when both parents look green. Colors have no fixed dominance ranking.

    This original teaching model has six interacting binary color genes and three numeric shade genes. Two genes form each color pathway; a pathway activates only when both inherited pairs are 1/1. With two parents carrying 0/1 at both genes, that pathway activates in 1 out of 16 possible transmissions. Several pathways can activate together, producing mixed colors. These are independent chances for each baby, not guaranteed proportions in a row of eight.

    Try an inheritance cross selects two green beetles with varied copies and turns mutation off. In the teacher lab, the offspring preview calculates color-family probabilities from all six genes. It weights the eight possible pathway patterns and 64 shade transmissions, grouping duplicate RGB colors. Students can see these details when full stats are enabled.

    Mutation is separate

    Unusual offspring do not require mutation if their parents already carry the necessary copies. A mutation can also introduce a new copy: a binary switch flips, or a shade value changes by ±1–36 within 0–255. The rate applies independently to 18 inherited copies. Mutations do not aim for the environment. Genes that are no longer carried by any beetle do not return from an invisible archive; new mutation would be needed.

    Bird mode, breeding mode, or both

    In bird mode, every living beetle gets one independent survival roll. The bird may eat any number, including none or all. Rows with two or more survivors produce eight offspring; one survivor remains on its own leaf without offspring, and zero survivors leaves an empty row. No survivor quota or rescue reroll is used. Contrasting beetles are more likely to be eaten when selection is strong, with chance still involved. Survival is sampled before the bird animation. At zero selection strength, each insect has a 70% survival chance regardless of color. With stronger selection, better camouflage improves that insect’s chance. At full strength, the survival probability is 10% + 80% × camouflage². Intermediate strength blends those two rules.

    In breeding mode, students choose parents, with no bird selection during that breeding step. When both activities are enabled, students may switch between choosing parents and watching natural selection. Jars always replace only their own rows. Turning off the bird animation hides the animation, while bird selection still operates.

    Neapolitan habitats

    Neapolitan is an additional environment alongside Meadow, Dry leaves, and Dusk. It divides the board’s leaf area into three continuous vertical color bands of equal width. Each insect is judged against the band behind its own leaf. With eight columns, the left three leaves are in the first third, the next two in the middle, and the right three in the last third. Jars are outside these survival zones. New offspring occupy their row’s leaf positions.

    Classroom links

    Classroom setup lets the teacher allow breeding only, bird only, or both, and choose whether students can change the environment. Shared links apply these permissions and starting presets automatically. The teacher may show full stats, charts, genetics, and data downloads, or keep the simplified view. Activity and environment permissions remain in effect. Start over restores the assigned starting conditions; notes remain.

    Observe and record

    Changing an environment never recolors existing beetles. It changes which colors blend in. Pause, predict, and compare the next generations. Average camouflage is a color similarity score, not a survival probability; diversity measures color spread. The teacher chart combines all rows, while each row tracks its own generation.

    A simplified model

    These invented interacting pathways illustrate inherited variation, gene interactions, natural selection, mutation, and drift. They do not claim to reproduce actual beetle color genetics. Each row has a capacity of eight, inheritance is independent between genes, and survival depends only on camouflage and chance.

    Drag with a mouse, trackpad, pen, or touch. With a keyboard, select a beetle and activate an empty jar slot. On small screens, swipe along a row. Save observations before leaving; all state stays in this browser tab.
